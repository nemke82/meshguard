How it works

From your phone to theirs, without the internet

MeshGuard is the app; Meshtastic radios carry the messages. Here is what happens to a message, who can see what, and what affects whether it arrives.

The path of a message

  1. You write a message. MeshGuard encrypts it on your phone for one contact.
  2. Your phone hands it to your radio over Bluetooth (or over your local Wi-Fi network, or a USB cable on a computer).
  3. Your radio transmits it over LoRa. If your contact’s radio is in range, it hears it directly.
  4. Other Meshtastic nodes may relay it. Depending on their settings, the hop limit and how busy the channel is, nodes in between can pass the packet on. They cannot read it.
  5. Your contact’s radio passes it to their phone, where MeshGuard decrypts it and sends back an encrypted confirmation.

Ways to reach your radio

None of these use the internet. They only connect your phone or computer to your own radio.

Bluetooth

The usual way on a phone. You pair the phone with the radio once, using the radio’s Bluetooth PIN.

Local Wi-Fi (TCP)

For radios with Wi-Fi on your home or field network. MeshGuard can find them on the network. This is a local connection, not internet access.

USB serial

On Linux and macOS, connect the radio with a USB cable.

Two separate layers of encryption

It helps to keep these apart: one protects your conversation, the other is part of the Meshtastic network.

MeshGuard end-to-end encryption

Each conversation has its own key that only you and your contact hold. Messages are encrypted with AES-256-GCM on your device and bound to the sender and the recipient. The key comes from a QR code you scan in person, or from a passphrase you both agreed on. It is never sent over the air.

Meshtastic channel encryption

Your radio also encrypts packets with its channel key. Everyone on the same channel shares that key, and the default channel key is publicly known. It keeps casual listeners out, but it is not what keeps your conversation private — MeshGuard’s own encryption does that.

What others can still see. Like any radio transmission, Meshtastic packets reveal metadata to radios in range: node numbers, when a packet was sent, its size and signal strength. MeshGuard hides what you say, not that your radio is transmitting. It does not make you anonymous.

What the delivery ticks mean

Each message you send shows one of these marks in the chat.

  • ⏳
    Sending
    Saved on your phone and waiting for the radio, or being sent again. Messages written while the radio is away are kept and sent when it is back.
  • ✓
    Sent
    Your radio has accepted the message for transmission. It does not yet mean your contact received it.
  • ✓✓
    Delivered
    Your contact’s MeshGuard received and decrypted it, and sent back an encrypted confirmation. Only in MeshGuard 2.0 mode.
  • ⚠
    Not delivered
    MeshGuard stopped trying: no confirmation came back after its last attempt, or the message reached the end of its lifetime.

A message can stay at one tick for a while: the confirmation has to travel back over the same mesh. See the FAQ for what to check.

What affects range

LoRa can cover long distances, but there is no single number that holds everywhere. What matters most:

  • Line of sight and terrain. Hills, buildings and trees block signals. Open ground or water carries much further than a city street.
  • Height and antenna. A radio held at waist height inside a pocket reaches far less than one on a windowsill or a roof.
  • Radio settings. Both radios must use the same region and modem preset. Long-range presets reach further but send more slowly.
  • Relays. Other Meshtastic nodes can extend the reach, if they are in range, configured to relay, and the hop limit allows it.
