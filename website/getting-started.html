Getting started

Your first offline message

This guide takes two people from unboxing to a confirmed message, using two Android phones and two Seeed SenseCAP T1000-E Meshtastic radios. It follows the current MeshGuard release; other radios work the same way.

On this page

  1. Before you begin
  2. Configure each radio
  3. Connect each phone
  4. Pair the two users
  5. Send the first message
  6. Check-in, diagnostics, SOS

Before you begin

  • Two Android phones (Android 8.0 or newer) with MeshGuard installed.
  • Two fully charged Seeed SenseCAP T1000-E radios running current Meshtastic firmware.
  • The official Meshtastic app, for the one-time radio setup in step A.
  • Bluetooth turned on. GPS on the phone is not needed; location for SOS comes from the radio.
  • Both people within radio reach of each other, directly or through mesh relays. Start in the same room for the first test.

No SIM card, mobile data or internet connection is needed to send messages between the radios.

AConfigure each Meshtastic radio

MeshGuard does not change radio settings. Do this once per radio in the official Meshtastic app, then close that app so it does not hold the Bluetooth connection.

  1. Region. Set the LoRa region that is legal where you are (for example EU_868 in Europe or US in North America). Both radios must use the same region.
  2. Modem preset. Use the same preset on both radios. The default, Long Fast, is a good start.
  3. Primary channel. Both radios must share the same primary channel name and key. The default channel works; a private channel keeps other Meshtastic users out of your traffic. MeshGuard sends on the primary channel.
  4. Bluetooth PIN. The T1000-E has no screen, so it uses a fixed Bluetooth PIN, 123456 by default. Note it if you changed it.

Connecting a phone to its radio over Bluetooth is not the same as pairing two MeshGuard users. Bluetooth links a phone to its own radio. MeshGuard pairing (step C) creates the encrypted conversation between two people.

BConnect each phone to its own T1000-E

Do this on both phones, each with its own radio.

  1. Open MeshGuard. On the Bluetooth tab, tap Scan for Devices.
  2. Android asks for permission to find nearby devices (on Android 11 and older it asks for location instead, which Android requires for Bluetooth scanning). Allow it. Allow notifications too, so you see incoming messages.
  3. Tap your T1000-E in the list. Check the Device PIN (default 123456) and connect. If Android shows its own Bluetooth pairing request, enter the same PIN.
  4. MeshGuard reads the radio and opens the Mesh Network screen. The header shows your radio’s name with (connected).

MeshGuard remembers the radio. Next time it reconnects on its own when you open the app, and again when the radio comes back after a restart or a dead battery. A first connection can take up to a minute while the radio sends its list of nodes; on a busy mesh this takes longer.

If the radio does not appear

  • Close the Meshtastic app or disconnect it there: a radio talks to one phone at a time.
  • Make sure the radio is on and charged, Bluetooth is on, and MeshGuard has the nearby-devices permission (Android settings → Apps → MeshGuard → Permissions).
  • Move closer and tap Scan for Devices again.

CPair the two MeshGuard users securely

Pairing creates a private key for your conversation. The QR code pairs the two users, not the radios. Do it in person, so you know whose code you are scanning.

  1. Phone A: on the Mesh Network screen, under Add a family member, tap Show my code. A QR code appears, valid for 10 minutes, with a text code underneath.
  2. Phone B: tap Scan code, allow the camera, and point it at phone A’s QR code.
  3. Phone B shows Request sent … waiting for confirmation. Phone A accepts it automatically while it shows the code, and shows Paired with and phone B’s name under the QR code. Wait for that line, then tap Done on phone A; tapping it earlier cancels the code.
  4. Both phones now list the other person under Chats, marked Encrypted Chat. Check that each phone shows the right name.

Other ways to pair

  • Type the code. If the camera is not available, MeshGuard offers to type the code shown under the QR instead.
  • Shared passphrase. If you cannot meet, agree on a passphrase of at least 8 characters by another channel. One person opens Find a node on the mesh, taps the other’s radio and enters the passphrase. The other person sees a chat request, taps Accept and enters the same passphrase. The passphrase is never stored or sent.
  • Several family members can scan the same QR code, one after another.

Never share screenshots of a pairing QR code or its text code. Anyone who scans it within its 10 minutes can pair with you.

DSend and confirm the first message

  1. On phone A, tap the contact under Chats and type a short message. The counter shows how many bytes are left (up to 173; emoji and accented letters use more than one).
  2. Send it and watch the mark next to it:
    • ⏳ Sending: saved and waiting for the radio.
    • ✓ Sent: your radio has it.
    • ✓✓ Delivered: phone B’s MeshGuard received and decrypted it.
  3. Phone B gets a notification and the message in the chat. Reply from phone B, and watch the same marks on that side.

Messages are saved on each phone, encrypted, until you delete the conversation. If your radio is out of reach when you send, the message waits and goes out when the radio is back.

ETry check-in, diagnostics and SOS

Check-in

In a chat, the buttons above the message box send a status with one tap: I’m OK, On my way, I need help and Share location. Tap the pencil to change the wording.

Diagnostics

Open Find a node on the mesh and tap the i next to a node. You see when it was last heard, hops away, signal (RSSI and SNR), battery and channel use when the node reports them, a signal history, and Run path test, which shows each hop a message takes.

Location and GPS

Location comes from the radio’s GPS, not the phone. The T1000-E has GPS built in, but it needs a view of the sky for its first fix, which can take several minutes. Without a fix, MeshGuard sends the check-in without a location and says so.

SOS

The red SOS button at the top of the Mesh Network screen asks for confirmation, then sends an urgent message with your location to all paired contacts. Their phones show a full-screen alert.

SOS only reaches your paired MeshGuard contacts. It does not contact emergency services, and delivery depends on radio range and relays. In an emergency, call your local emergency number if you can.

Troubleshooting

Bluetooth discovery fails

  • Allow the nearby-devices permission (location on Android 11 and older), and turn Bluetooth on.
  • Charge the radio. Disconnect it in the Meshtastic app or any other phone; it connects to one phone at a time.
  • If pairing with the PIN failed before, remove the radio in Android’s Bluetooth settings and connect again.

The radios are connected, but messages do not arrive

  • Check that both radios use the same region, modem preset and primary channel (name and key).
  • Check that both phones show each other under Chats. If one side removed the contact, pair again.
  • Move closer, or higher. Use Run path test to see whether the other radio is reachable.

A message stays at one tick

One tick means your radio accepted it, not that it arrived. The confirmation needs to travel back over the mesh. If the other phone is out of reach, or the app on it is closed, the second tick comes later or not at all. MeshGuard 2.0 resends it a few times, with growing pauses, until it is confirmed; after the last attempt, or when its lifetime ends, it is marked ⚠. Contacts paired with MeshGuard 2.0 mode turned off never show two ticks.

No location in a check-in or SOS

The radio has no GPS fix yet, or its last fix is old. Take the radio outside with a view of the sky and wait a few minutes. Make sure GPS is enabled in the radio’s Meshtastic settings.

App closed or phone locked

Today MeshGuard receives messages while the app is running. If Android stops it in the background, or you close it, messages for you are not shown until you open it again. A 2.0 sender resends a few times before giving up, so some of them may still arrive later. Background messaging and replying from a notification are in development.
